# Pricing notes: 69p

## Setting the price

- In the Play Console go to **Monetise › Products › App pricing**, choose *Paid* and set **GBP 0.69** for the United Kingdom.
- UK Play prices **include VAT**. Google collects and pays the 20% VAT for UK sales, so you don't.
- For other countries, either let Play convert from the GBP price, or set round local prices: for example US$0.99, €0.79, A$1.49, CA$1.29. Play converts once when you set the price and won't change it later when exchange rates move.
- **An app can switch from paid to free, but never from free back to paid.** Start paid.

## What you keep per UK sale (approximate)

| | |
|---|---|
| Price paid | £0.69 |
| Less 20% VAT (0.69 ÷ 1.2) | £0.575 |
| Less Google's 15% service fee (on the first US$1m of earnings each year) | ≈ £0.49 |

So it's roughly **49p per UK sale**. 100 sales ≈ £49 and 1,000 sales ≈ £490. Check the current fee rate in the Play Console before relying on these numbers.

## Why 69p works here

- It's under the "think about it" threshold, and below the €1.39–1.79 of the few paid watch games I found.
- There are no ads and no in-app purchases, so the price *is* the business model. Say so in the listing. "Pay once, no ads" is a real selling point on a 1.4-inch screen.
- A short launch discount (Play supports temporary sales) is a cheap way to get first reviews.

## Things you need before you can charge

1. A **Google Play developer account** (one-off US$25 fee).
2. A **payments profile / merchant account** linked to the developer account, needed for paid apps.
3. **New personal developer accounts** must run a **closed test with at least 12 testers for 14 days** before production access. Ask friends with Wear OS watches; testers can be added by email or Google Group. Check the current numbers in the Play Console, as Google changes this rule from time to time.
